What the columns mean (tap to open)
Proj
Predicted fantasy points this week: the average of ESPN's projection, Sleeper's projection and what a player at his expert-consensus rank usually scores, blended (up to half weight) with the matchup view — his own per-game production scaled by how generous this week's defense really is to his position, from actual box scores — then nudged by the Vegas line and his chance of playing. The consensus is slow to reprice a broken or surging situation; the matchup view catches it.
Next 4
His team's next four opponents, each with the fantasy points that defense actually gives up per game to his position. Green = soft matchup coming, red = stingy. Early season this runs on few games, so treat middling numbers as noise.
Floor–Ceil
A bad week and a good week for him (20th and 80th percentile). Wide range = boom-or-bust.
Wk rank
FantasyPros expert consensus rank for this week. QB, K and DST are ranked within their position; RB/WR/TE are ranked together (that's the flex pool). Lower is better.
Opp
This week's opponent. "vs." = home, "at" = away.
Grade
FantasyPros start/sit letter grade for this week (A = start with confidence, D/F = sit).
ROS rank
Rest-of-season rank across ALL positions (FantasyPros, refreshed Tuesdays). This is the season-long value number. Lower is better.
Season pts
ESPN's live projection of his total fantasy points for the whole season. Second season-long signal.
Bye
The week his NFL team doesn't play.
Status
Injury tag (Q = questionable, D = doubtful, OUT, IR) with his chance of playing; "locked" = his game already kicked off; BYE = off this week.
Odds (start/sit)
Chance the bench player outscores the starter he would replace. Under 35% leave it; 35–55% coin flip, check news; over 55% the tool already swapped him in.
Title % (league)
The ranking. Chance a team wins the championship, from 10,000 simulated seasons: every remaining matchup on the real schedule, then the playoff bracket exactly as the league settings define it (playoff teams, seeding tiebreaker, reseeding). Playoff % = chance it makes the bracket; Proj seed = its average final standing (1 = top seed).
Scoring rate (league)
A team's expected points per week from here on: a blend of its actual weekly average and its roster talent converted to points. Early on talent carries more weight; every real week shifts the weight toward actual scoring (the % in brackets). The blend weight is measured from the league's own scores, not chosen.
Roster talent (ROS)
Paper strength: sum over a team's 9 best starters of (420 − rest-of-season rank), so +10 ≈ upgrading one starter by 10 rank spots. It is an input to the scoring rate — it ignores record and real scoring, so it never ranks teams on its own. "This week" = that team's projected points with its best lineup.
Title Δ (trades, waivers, start/sit)
How much a move changes your title %, re-simulated with both rosters' talent updated (same random seasons, so the difference is the move itself). Shown in percentage points (pp). Talent you / them = the roster-talent change on each side — the other manager judges the offer on that, so it drives the odds they accept.
EV (trades)
Title Δ × the chance they say yes. The one number to sort trades by.
Win odds (matchup)
Chance your best lineup outscores theirs this week, from both teams' predicted totals and the usual week-to-week swing of every player involved.
All-play (matchup)
What a team's record would be if it played everyone every week. Strips the schedule out: a 1-2 team with a 12-4 all-play has been good and unlucky.
Luck (matchup)
Actual wins minus the wins the all-play record says the scores deserved. Positive = the schedule has been kind; negative = better than the record shows.
